但我们仍然听到某种谈判的回声，那是通过巴基斯坦外长和巴基斯坦总理间接进行的。然后在6点刚过时，总统宣布，他再次在这里叫停。突然之间，停火出现了，一个新的两周期限也出现了。从市场投资者到军方，再到海湾周边的许多人，所有人都长长地松了一口气。

## rachel abrams
We’ll be right back. So David, as you described, this moment of ceasefire seems to be this huge relief. But how real does this deal actually feel? And what does it actually mean?
我们稍后回来。David，正如你描述的那样，这个停火时刻似乎带来了巨大的缓解。但这项协议到底有多真实？它实际上意味着什么？

## david sanger
Well, it could mean the end of this war or it could mean just a brief pause. Ceasefires are fragile by nature. We’re going to have to see if anybody violates it. And even if they have the discipline to keep it in place, we have to determine if the traffic that’s going through the Strait of Hormuz is getting back to what it was before February 28, when the fighting broke out. And, of course, we’re going to be in a situation now where the Iranian military actually still controls who’s going through the strait. That wasn’t the case back in February.
它可能意味着这场战争的结束，也可能只是一段短暂的暂停。停火本质上就是脆弱的。我们必须观察是否有人违反停火。即便双方有纪律维持停火，我们也必须判断通过霍尔木兹海峡的船舶流量是否能恢复到2月28日战斗爆发前的水平。当然，我们现在所处的局面是，伊朗军方实际上仍然控制着谁可以通过海峡。而在2月份时，情况并不是这样。

Right.

## david sanger
For the president, he said, basically, it’s got to go back to the way it was. But the fact of the matter is, the Iranians have now discovered this is their greatest leverage. And it’s not a power they are likely to give up anytime soon.
对总统来说，他的意思基本上是，一切必须恢复到原来的样子。但事实是，伊朗人现在已经发现，这是他们最大的杠杆。而这种权力，他们不太可能很快放弃。

## rachel abrams
So was that the biggest test of the ceasefire, whether we see all these ships going through the strait?
所以，这是不是停火面临的最大考验，也就是我们是否能看到所有这些船只重新通过海峡？

## david sanger
That’s the immediate test of the ceasefire. But remember, the ceasefire is only the means to an end. And its purpose is to allow the time and space to go negotiate on the bigger issues. It took two and a half years, Rachel, to negotiate the 2015 agreement between the Obama administration and the Iranians. And that was in peace time. 这是停火面临的即时考验。但要记住，停火只是实现目标的手段。它的目的是为谈判更大的问题争取时间和空间。Rachel，当年奥巴马政府与伊朗谈判2015年协议，用了两年半时间。而那还是在和平时期。你可以想象，在伊朗人认为这场战争让他们更有底气的时刻，现在谈判会有多么困难。

## rachel abrams
To that point, at the outset of the war, the US said that one of its big reasons for going to war was, in fact, among other reasons, keep Iran from making a nuclear weapon. That feels very absent from these discussions. Why is that?
说到这一点，在战争开始时，美国曾表示，除了其他原因外，参战的一个重要理由其实是阻止伊朗制造核武器。但在这些讨论中，这一点似乎非常缺席。为什么会这样？

## david sanger
Well, the nuclear weapons issue is, presumably, the first issue they’re going to have to take up in these negotiations over the next two weeks. And it’s going to be hard. And the reason it’s going to be hard is, first of all, the president himself has been all over the map here. Before the war broke out, he said the United States cannot tolerate Iran having any nuclear material, any of its stockpile left in the country. It all had to go. Then about a week and a half ago, he said, I don’t really care. It’s buried so deep, we can watch it by satellite. And now it’s likely he’s back to the negotiating position they were taking before the war, which is, Iran cannot be left with any nuclear material. And if the president left a significant amount of near bomb-grade uranium in Iran, he would be getting less out of the Iranians than Barack Obama got out of them in 2015. And frankly, it’s hard to imagine that the president wants to read that he went to war with Iran and didn’t get the material that was the reason for this conflict at the start.
核武器问题大概会是他们在未来两周谈判中必须首先处理的问题。而这会很难。之所以难，首先是因为总统本人在这个问题上的立场一直摇摆不定。战争爆发前，他说美国不能容忍伊朗境内保留任何核材料、任何库存，所有这些都必须移出。然后大约一周半前，他又说，我其实不在乎。它埋得那么深，我们可以通过卫星监控。现在，他很可能又回到了战前他们原本采取的谈判立场，也就是伊朗境内不能留下任何核材料。如果总统允许伊朗境内保留大量接近武器级的铀，那他从伊朗人那里得到的东西将少于 Barack Obama 在2015年得到的成果。坦率地说，很难想象总统愿意看到这样的评价：他和伊朗开了一场战，却没有拿到最初导致这场冲突的那批材料。

## rachel abrams
Well, that makes me wonder about the effectiveness of these talks overall and how we should be thinking about them. The threat to annihilate a whole country, is that something that brought them to the table for a meaningful negotiations to end the war and get demands that the United States actually wanted? Or is this ceasefire just a temporary de-escalation that serves to save face more than anything else?
这让我想知道，这些谈判整体上到底有多有效，我们又应该如何看待它们。威胁毁灭整个国家，这是否真的把伊朗人带到了谈判桌前，促成了有意义的谈判，从而结束战争并让美国获得自己真正想要的东西？还是说，这次停火只是一种临时降温，更多是为了保全面子？

## david sanger
It gains nothing we didn’t have before the war began. But I think you have to give credit to the president, because this ratcheting up of pressure certainly played some role in forcing an agreement. It’s not clear whether or not it actually terrified the Iranians, because their decision-making process is so spread out among the military and the clerics and so forth. It’s not clear who was making these decisions. But it certainly scared the region. And I think it scared the Chinese, who were highly dependent on oil moving through the strait. So there were pressure points that the president was able to take advantage of.
它并没有让我们获得战争开始前没有的东西。但我认为你必须承认，总统不断升级施压，确实在迫成协议方面发挥了一定作用。现在还不清楚这是否真的吓到了伊朗人，因为他们的决策过程分散在军方、神职人员等不同群体之间。我们并不清楚到底是谁在做这些决定。但它确实吓到了整个地区。我认为它也吓到了中国，因为中国高度依赖通过这条海峡运输的石油。所以，总统确实利用了一些压力点。

## rachel abrams
David, on the off chance that this temporary ceasefire leads to a permanent ceasefire, what will the White House, what will the United States have actually gained from all of this?
David，假设这次临时停火真的发展成永久停火，那么白宫、美国到底从这一切中实际得到了什么？

## david sanger
Look, I think the United States and the Israelis have accomplished a lot. They took out, in the opening hours of the war, the supreme leader. They devastated levels of the military and intelligence and nuclear establishment. They set back the missile program and the missile production program. But if this ceasefire essentially becomes permanent, with no change in Iran beyond the restoration of traffic in the Strait of Hormuz, then the White House will have accomplished virtually none of its major goals. The country is still run by a military and theocratic group of elites who have terrorized their own population. They still have their nuclear material and could, in theory, try to go rebuild and race for a bomb. They still have missile supplies and drones. Not on the scale that they did before the attacks, but we’ve learned that they could rebuild fairly quickly. And most importantly, I think, they still have a sense that they were able to stand up to the United States and Israel and resist against a much larger invading force.
你看，我认为美国和以色列确实取得了不少成果。在战争最初几个小时里，他们除掉了最高领袖。他们重创了伊朗军事、情报和核体系的多个层级。他们也让伊朗的导弹项目和导弹生产项目倒退了。但如果这次停火实际上变成永久停火，而伊朗除了恢复霍尔木兹海峡通行之外没有发生其他变化，那么白宫几乎没有实现任何主要目标。这个国家仍然由一群军事和神权精英统治，他们一直恐吓自己的人民。伊朗仍然拥有核材料，理论上仍可能尝试重建并冲刺制造核弹。他们仍有导弹和无人机库存。规模已经不如袭击前那样大，但我们已经知道，他们重建速度可能相当快。最重要的是，我认为，他们仍然会有一种感觉：他们能够顶住美国和以色列，抵抗一支强大得多的入侵力量。

## rachel abrams
David, we’ve talked on the show, quite a bit, about how you can’t put everything back together that has been broken by this war. David，我们在节目里已经多次谈到，这场战争打碎的一切，不可能都重新拼回原样。也就是说，即便战争今晚结束，或者两周后结束，经济影响、政治影响也不会就此回到原状。如此多的基础设施被摧毁，如此多的事情已经发生，使得这些影响可能在未来多年持续扩散。所以我想知道，具体来说，在你看来，此时此刻有哪些后果似乎会长期存在？

## david sanger
Well, a few things. First of all, the Iranians have discovered that they have a greater power than they knew over commerce around the world. Second, the Gulf states feel more vulnerable. And I think there’s a last thing we’ve learned.
有几件事。首先，伊朗人发现，自己对全球贸易拥有比他们以为的更大影响力。其次，海湾国家感到更加脆弱。他们建起了这些宏大、美丽、充满摩天大楼的城市。他们进入了21世纪，然后他们发现了什么？他们发现伊朗人可以把导弹打进他们的窗户，打进他们的海水淡化厂。世界经济也显得更加脆弱，因为我们又发现了一个供应链脆弱点。我们还发现，美国曾经以为自己又一次摆脱了中东——这正是去年秋天总统国家安全战略中明确提出的目标——但事实上，美国又被卷了回去，而且卷入的规模，是几个月前几乎没人能想象的。我认为，我们还学到了最后一件事。

The world never really approved of the amount of power the United States has had, but it’s usually signed up to the thought that the US was fundamentally a benevolent superpower in the past. And yet, I think we lost a lot of that reputation in this attack on Iran. That there was a sense that the president was never committed to the February diplomacy, that there was a sense that he thought he could topple a weak regime, that he could go out and threaten the lives of 92 million people, and that the threat came from a president who oversees the world’s most powerful military. And so eventually, gas prices will come down. And eventually, oil will flow more naturally. And over time, the damage of the war will be rebuilt the way it was in Japan and Germany and Vietnam. But whether the world ever views the United States quite the way it did before this happened, I’m not sure that’s going to be back for a long time.
世界从未真正认可美国所拥有的巨大权力，但过去通常还是接受这样一种看法：美国本质上是一个相对善意的超级大国。然而，我认为，在这次对伊朗的袭击中，我们失去了很多这样的声誉。外界有一种感觉，认为总统从来没有真正致力于2月份的外交；也有一种感觉，认为他以为自己可以推翻一个脆弱的政权；他可以走出来威胁9200万人的生命，而且这种威胁来自一位掌管世界最强大军队的总统。所以，最终汽油价格会下降。最终，石油会更自然地流动。随着时间推移，战争造成的破坏也会像日本、德国和越南那样被重建起来。但世界是否还会像这件事发生之前那样看待美国，我不确定那会在很长时间内恢复。
